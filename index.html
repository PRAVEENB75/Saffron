Problem Statement

Saffron (Crocus sativus) is the world's most valuable spice, requiring extremely strict climatic conditions not found in most regions:

  • High summer temperatures during sprouting destroy corms.
  • Unstable humidity causes fungal rot in corm tissue.
  • Lack of cold environment (15–18°C) prevents flowering.
  • Geographical restrictions limit natural growth to specific regions.

Proposed IoT Solution

An automated artificial growing chamber maintaining perfect microclimate conditions year-round:

  • DHT11/22 Sensor: Continuous environmental telemetry.
  • Ultrasonic Fogger & Fans: IF-ELSE threshold controls.
  • Acoustic Wave Tuning: Sound frequency for flavonoid stimulation.
  • Firebase & ThingSpeak: Realtime cloud data & mobile app dashboard.

3-Layer System Architecture

1

Hardware Layer

  • ESP32 / Arduino Microcontroller
  • DHT11 / DHT22 Temp & Humidity Sensor
  • IRFZ44N N-Channel MOSFET Modules
  • Relay Switch Board & Buzzer
  • PWM Purple LED Light Strip
  • Ultrasonic Mist Maker (Fogger)
  • Cooler Fan & Thermocol Ice Box
2

Cloud Layer

  • ThingSpeak Platform: Realtime Data Visualization & Charts
  • Firebase Realtime Database: Cloud Sync & Actuator Control State
3

Application Layer

  • Android Mobile Application: User Interface dashboard
  • Light intensity & frequency cycle sliders
  • Realtime alerts & crop recommendation notifications
Agricultural Research

Saffron Growth Science & Climate Thresholds

Scientific findings on optimal growth temperatures, soil conditions, CO₂ enrichment, and acoustic stimulation.

Stage 1

Germination & Early Growth

15°C – 20°C

Bulb planting and initial root emergence. Soil must stay cool to avoid bulb stress.

Stage 2

Vegetative Growth

18°C – 22°C

Leaf expansion and root network development. Requires mild weather away from heat/frost.

Stage 3

Flowering & Bloom

15°C – 18°C

Most critical phase. Cold stimulus triggers vivid purple bloom and red stigma formation.

Stage 4

Harvesting & Senescence

16°C – 18°C

Flowers picked at dawn. Corms rest and multiply into daughter cells from May to August.

CO₂ Management (400–600 ppm)

Elevated carbon dioxide levels in controlled greenhouses accelerate photosynthesis, leading to larger corms and up to 35% higher stigma weight yield per flower.

Acoustic Wave Stimulation

Sound vibrations trigger physiological secondary metabolites. Playing 432Hz/528Hz acoustic frequencies promotes flavonoid production and speeds up flowering response.
